The two profiles

Who builds AI and who builds with AI

Neither profile is better than the other. They are two ways to get started, and companies want them working together.

🛠️ AI Engineer

Works inside the machine. Builds, trains, and maintains AI models, algorithms, and the systems that make everything run for millions of people.

Typically comes from computer science, software engineering, mathematics, statistics, physics, or a related field. The selection process includes a technical interview.

+

🧩 AI Builder

Works at the business end. Uses AI to create automations, agents, and products that solve a real problem in a field, from start to finish.

Can come from any educational background: business, marketing, economics, design, law. No in-depth programming is needed. The selection process includes a business case.

Which one are you? Answer honestly

You may lean toward Engineer if…

  • when something breaks, you want to open it up and find out why;
  • you enjoy math, logic, and clean code;
  • you prefer a hard technical problem to a meeting with another team;
  • you've worked (or want to work) with models, data, and servers.

You may lean toward Builder if…

  • you quickly spot what's slowing down the people around you;
  • you like talking with people, understanding their pain points, and persuading them to try something;
  • you want a solution working tomorrow, even if it's simple;
  • you already use AI to make presentations, analyze spreadsheets, or automate tasks.

Marked answers on both sides? Great: choose the side you'd apply for today and use the other as a strength in your interview. The prompt in Get started runs through this test with you, one question at a time.

📖 Words you’ll come across

  • Agent: an AI that carries out tasks on its own (searches, fills in forms, sends things), rather than just responding.
  • Automation: a repetitive task that starts happening on its own.
  • Vibe coding: creating an app or website by chatting with AI, which writes the code.
  • Prompt: the request you write for the AI.
  • Business case: a company problem that you analyze and solve in front of an evaluator.
  • Residency: the program for recent graduates.
  • Bootcamp and hackathon: short events (from one to a few days) to learn or build a project as a group.

The plan

12 weeks, alongside your regular schedule

About 5 hours per week, by our estimate. Each phase ends with something you can show.

1

Weeks 1 and 2: choose a profile and use AI every day basics

Take the test for both profiles. Then choose 3 tasks you already do every week (for school or work) and start doing them with AI. Write down how long they took before and how long they take now: that's your first metric.

To get started from scratch: From chat to agent and Which AI to use.

2

Weeks 3 to 6: follow your profile's learning path learning path

Builder path: Integrate Your Profession with AI → Strategic Automation with AI → AI Strategy Factory.

Engineer path: Build an Agent System from Scratch → AI Agent Engineering → Field AI Engineer (the part about proving it with measured tests).

Courses on these learning paths are in Portuguese.

3

Weeks 7 to 10: build a proof project portfolio

Build one real project: an agent or automation that solves someone's pain point (your team, an NGO, a relative's company, your college department). Agree in advance on which metric to change, deliver it, ask that person to use it, and measure again two weeks later.

Write a page describing the problem, what you did, the before-and-after metric, and what went wrong. That's what you'll show during the selection process.

4

Weeks 11 and 12: practice for the selection process practice

Go through each selection step with AI playing the evaluator, several times, until you can tell your story and describe your project without reading (see the selection process).

5

Until the next cohort: one project a month keep it up

After the 12 weeks, keep up the pace: one small project a month, each with a metric. The 2nd edition opened applications in late October; if there is a 3rd edition, it's reasonable to expect something similar in 2027 (that's our estimate, not an announcement). Keep an eye on the careers page.

The selection process

The 6 steps and how to practice each one

This is the process iFood described in the genAI video presentation (the official announcement doesn’t detail the stages). Other AI programs use similar stages.

1. Online application

Resume and form. Practice: ask AI to review your resume as if it were the recruiter and remove anything that doesn't show what you did.

2. AI interview on WhatsApp

A bot asks questions to get to know you beyond your resume. Practice: ask AI to do the same through short messages, and answer as you would on your phone, with real examples.

3. Online challenge

Logical reasoning and other skills. Practice: ask for timed logic problems and have it explain each mistake.

4. Recruiter conversation

Who you are and why you want this program. Practice: tell your story in 2 minutes: where you came from, what you've built, and why you.

5. Technical interview or case

Engineer: technical questions and problem-solving. Builder: a business case. Practice: AI plays the evaluator and gives you a vague problem; ask questions before you suggest a solution.

6. Conversation with the team lead

The person who will welcome you to the team. Practice: present your proof project as if they were the client: the problem, the metric, and what went wrong.

Two tips the program itself repeated: be yourself, without putting on an act, and show how your curiosity leads to real work.

Common questions

Before you get started

Applications are closed. Is it still worth it?

It's even more useful now: if you prepare months in advance, you'll have a finished project ready. And the plan works for other programs and regular AI jobs.

I’m from a humanities background. Do I have a chance?

Yes: the Builder profile is open to any field, as long as you meet the graduation timeline (internship) or recent graduate timeline (residency) and have experience with prompts, vibe coding, and automations—skills the plan helps you build. In the program presentation, iFood said there’s even a nurse in the first cohort.

I graduated more than a year ago.

For genAI, the residency is for people who graduated within the past year. The plan still applies to other jobs, a career change, or using AI in your current work.

Do I need to speak English?

According to the announcement for the 2nd edition, English isn't required to apply to genAI. But it helps a lot with day-to-day work and the international placement.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. The courses in the plan and this guide are free. The free versions of ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini are enough for practice.

I live far from São Paulo.

According to the 2nd edition announcement, the Engineer track is remote. The Builder track is hybrid for people in Osasco (SP) and remote for people in other cities. There are in-person meetups at times such as the start of the program.
